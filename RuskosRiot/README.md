# Rusko's Riot — Food Fight
### Level 1 vertical slice: Billy the Bully

Drop this folder's contents next to your original PNGs and open `index.html`
with Live Server. No build step, no dependencies, no bundler — plain
HTML/CSS/JS with classic `<script>` tags, so it also runs off `file://`.

---

## The one mechanic everything hangs off

**The lunch tray is the shield, and it moves.** Tray up (the default) and you
are safe — anything Billy throws splatters across it and slides off. Pull back
to aim and the tray drops out of the way, and that is the only moment he can
actually hit you.

No duck button, and nothing hidden: `player.exposed` is literally
`tray.raise < TRAY_SAFE`, so what protects you is exactly what you can see on
screen. The tray drops in 0.13 s (you commit to a shot) but takes 0.34 s to
come back up — **that lag is the entire risk of shooting.** Get caught in it
and you wear a banana.

So Billy's `ready_to_throw` pose isn't a prompt to press anything. It's a
~1 second warning to take the shot you've got or let go and get back behind
the tray.

| Input | Aim + power | Fire | Get behind the tray |
|---|---|---|---|
| Touch / mouse | drag away from your start point | release | release (tray auto-raises) |
| Keyboard | hold `SPACE` to charge, `←` `→` to steer | release `SPACE` | `ESC` or `S` |

`D` toggles a debug overlay: slot IDs, scale factors, hit boxes and the live
hit band.

## The aim arrow

A yellow arrow traces the front portion of your shot while you draw. It is
generated by walking the **same integration the live projectile uses**, so it
can never disagree with where the food actually goes.

How much of the flight it reveals is per-level, so your aim improves as the
game gets harder:

| Level | `aimArc` | what you see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Billy | 0.28 | a short stub off the slingshot; you are still guessing the landing |
| 2 — Mrs. Gabel | 0.45 | most of the climb |
| 3 — Coach Ken | 0.65 | the apex and the start of the fall |
| 4 — Principal Dan | 0.90 | effectively the whole arc |

One number in `config.js` per level. Set it to 1.0 if you want the full arc
while play-testing.

| Category | What changed |
|---|---|

| Characters | cropped to a **shared union box per character** so every pose keeps the same foot line and relative height, then scaled to 0.72 px per design unit |

## Design units

Every measurement in `config.js` is in the coordinate space of the
1084 × 2340 background. The canvas always renders at that internal
resolution and is CSS-scaled to fit the device, so **you can re-export any
PNG at any resolution and nothing in the game code changes** — the manifest
carries each sprite's design size separately from its pixel size.

## Perspective

The background is drawn in *cartoon* perspective. I solved the true
vanishing point off the maroon wainscot lines — it lands at (565, 1184) —
but the back-wall door is drawn about 4× larger than real geometry allows.
So the scale ramp is calibrated to the **artwork**, not the maths:

```
scale(floorY) = (floorY - 927) / 1413
```

A kid at the back reads ~390 px, up close ~745 px. Change `HORIZON` and
`SPAN` in `config.js` if you want the room deeper or shallower.

## Ballistics, and why power matters

The ball tracks `(fy, h, wx)`: the floor point it's over, its height above
that point, and its lateral offset. Screen position is derived, so the arc
shrinks automatically as it flies into the room.

Measured off the live physics — height on arrival, cover at 381/411,
Billy's head at 1080:

| power | lands at | h @ NC (1900) | h @ ML/MR (1680) | h @ FL/FR (1450) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 1741 | 385 | — | — |
| 0.65 | 1453 | 660 | 484 | — |
| 0.80 | 1297 | 795 | 839 | 670 |
| 0.90 | 1300 | 850 | 982 | 953 |
| 1.00 | 1297 | 892 | 1086 **over** | 1152 **over** |

Near is forgiving (p ≥ 0.50), mid needs 0.63–0.97, far needs 0.73–0.97, and
**full power sails over his head at both back tiers** — so mashing max draw
is punished. Verified at 30/30 on solved aim shots across all five slots.

## Scoring

Base 100 × depth (far ×2.0, mid ×1.5, near ×1.0). Head shot ×2. Combo
+10% per consecutive hit up to ×3, broken by a miss (no point penalty).
Quick Draw +250 inside 1.2 s of the pop-up. **Peel Slip +500.** Level end:
Flawless 5000, Untouched 2500, +10/second left on the clock.

**Peels:** a missed banana that lands near a slot stays there. Next time
Billy pops up at that slot he goes over on it — free damage, big bonus, and
your misses stop feeling wasted.

## The slingshot

`slingshot_alpha.png` is drawn as a sprite; only the rubber, the pouch and the
loaded food are vector. The two band anchors were solved off the artwork's
**alpha channel** (centroid of each prong knob) rather than eyeballed, and are
stored as fractions of the sprite in `CFG.SLING`:

```
anchorL: [0.111, 0.072]
anchorR: [0.883, 0.072]
```

Re-export the fork with different framing and you only need to re-solve those
two pairs — the rubber will still land exactly on the knobs at any size.
The rig hangs from its prong line so the anchors stay put as it leans, the
bands thin as they stretch, and the whole thing tilts up to 0.075 rad into the
shot. The aim arrow starts at the pouch so it reads as leaving the sling.

**Worth knowing:** the source art is 200 x 324 px, which is small next to the
rest of the set (characters are 896 x 1200). It renders at 280 design units
wide, so it is being upscaled about 1.45x and is very slightly soft. It holds
up because it is a smooth wooden object with no fine detail — but if you have
the source, a 600 px-wide re-export would be pin sharp. Drop it in the folder
and I will re-run the pipeline; nothing in the game code needs to change.

## Layering: what "blocked" looks like

Billy's food and the mess it makes are drawn **under** the tray, so a raised
tray physically occludes them. The banana grows toward the camera, disappears
behind the rim, and the splat detonates behind the shield — only the spray
clears the top edge. Nothing is ever painted onto the tray face, because food
sitting *on* your shield reads as a hit, not a block.

Render order in `render()`:

```
drawScene()      floor, cover, enemy, your food in flight
drawInbound()    Billy's food   <- under the tray
drawTrayHits()   the spray      <- under the tray
drawTray()       the shield     <- occludes both
drawSling()      only while the tray is down
```

## Tray tuning

| Knob | Now | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `TRAY_DOWN` | 0.13 s | how fast the tray drops when you start a draw |
| `TRAY_UP` | 0.34 s | how long you stay uncovered after releasing |
| `TRAY_SAFE` | 0.55 | raise value at which the tray starts counting as cover |

Measured recovery after release: `0.29 → 0.59 → 0.88 → 1.0` over ~240 ms, so
you are genuinely open for about 0.16 s past the shot. Raise `TRAY_UP` to make
shooting scarier, lower it to make the game kinder.

## Wired but not switched on

`config.js` already defines cookie / tomato / watermelon (ricochet, splash,
cover-breaking). Adding levels 2–4 means appending to `CFG.LEVELS` — the
engine reads `char`, `hp`, `aimArc` and the timing block generically.

**Own Medicine** (`ownMedicine: true`) doubles damage when you hit an enemy
with their own signature food. It's off for level 1 because banana-vs-Billy
would double every single shot; it comes alive from Mrs. Gabel onward, where
the food you just unlocked is always the current enemy's own.

The shirt-loss handler in `game.js` has the marked hook where a "buy a clean
shirt to continue" ad would slot in.
